Sim vs live gaps

  • Latency. Spec targets under 10 ms. This box measured a WebSocket p50 of 48 ms and a p99 of 173 ms (server timestamp to receive, which includes clock skew), and each check only looks for about 75 s every 15 minutes. Real arbitrage is won by colocated bots.
  • No Kalshi WebSocket. Kalshi's live feed needs an authenticated API key, so Kalshi books come from REST snapshots. Kalshi legs are never quoted as maker, only taken against a fresh re-fetch.
  • Maker-fill uncertainty. A maker fill only counts when a trade we saw went through our price, or past the queue ahead of us. Queue position, hidden size and cancellations are guesses, and nothing counts when we weren't watching.
  • Legging risk. Taker legs fill on two separate fresh re-fetches, several hundred ms apart. If the second leg fails, the first is sold back at the bid and the loss is booked. Live legging could be better (faster) or worse (others react to our first fill).
  • Resolution risk. Partition and ladder trades assume each venue resolves exactly as its rules say. Disputes (UMA on Polymarket), ambiguous wording, 50-50 splits or venue cancellation can break the guarantee. Cross-venue pairs are never traded until a human verifies the rules.
  • Depth competition. Only 50% of each displayed level is assumed fillable. Real books can disappear entirely within milliseconds of a public mispricing.
  • Capital and access. The sim treats $1,000 as one pool, but live trading would need separate funded accounts on each venue, and transfer and bridge costs aren't modelled. Check that Polymarket and Kalshi accept accounts from Alberta before any real money goes in.
  • Marking. Equity marks open positions at what they'd sell for right now at the bid, after fees, so a correctly hedged arb can still show a temporary loss until it resolves or converges.
